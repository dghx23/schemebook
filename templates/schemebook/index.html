Independent of any scheme, broker, or insurer. SchemeBook does not sell cover and does not advise you on which plan to choose — it organises the public record (CMS-registered schemes, Prescribed Minimum Benefits, the Chronic Disease List, and Diagnosis Treatment Pairs) and explains the jargon in plain language.

What

A registry and a translator, in one place

SchemeBook is two things bolted together: a directory of every CMS-registered medical scheme in South Africa (open and restricted, with type and PMB coverage), and a plain-language glossary of the terms those schemes' rules assume you already know — co-payment, DSP, late-joiner penalty, and the rest. It is a reference tool, not a comparison or quote engine.

Why

The rules are public. Reading them isn't easy.

Prescribed Minimum Benefits, the Chronic Disease List, and Diagnosis Treatment Pairs are all matters of public record — but they're published as government gazettes and 71 separate scheme rule documents, in regulatory language nobody outside the industry was taught to read. SchemeBook exists so that gap isn't the reason a claim gets refused, a benefit goes unused, or a reform proposal has to start from zero.

Where

Its own address

Standalone at schemebook.riskatlas.co.za, under the ZA portfolio — its own front door, not a page buried inside something else.

When

Before the jargon costs you something

Before you sign up or switch schemes, when a claim or a co-payment surprises you, when a rejection or scheme letter uses a term you don't recognise, or when you're researching the system itself rather than your own cover. The underlying PMB data refreshes on a daily cache cycle from the Council for Medical Schemes; the scheme registry follows the CMS Industry Report as new editions publish.
